# My Banker Serbia — Call Notes 2

## Five questions for the meeting

These questions are designed to test the mechanism, not to criticise the ambition. The ambition can remain: a more proactive financial experience in Serbia. The question is whether peer ownership is the right mechanism for credit.

---

## 1. What actually triggers credit demand?

> Before we discuss peer analysis, I want us to look at real client behaviour. Take the last 50 credits issued in this segment and write down what triggered each application. Was it a car, a renovation, a medical expense, refinancing, short-term liquidity, or something else?
>
> Then show me how many were triggered because a client learned that people with similar age and income already held the same product.
>
> If that number is close to zero, then peer comparison is not the trigger through which credit demand happens in our client base. Credit is triggered by an event or a need. The question is not whether we can show a percentage; the question is why we are building the experience around a trigger that is absent from actual client behaviour.

**If they say:** “The feature can create new interest.”

> If the client already had a need, how does the percentage help them make a better choice? If they did not have a need, what concrete client benefit does the new interest create compared with a normal credit campaign?

---

## 2. What decision does the peer percentage actually make?

> On the screen, we know the client’s age, income band, and what products similar clients hold. Then we show that 60% have Cash Loan or 90% have Credit Card.
>
> What decision does that number make for this individual client?
>
> It tells us that a product is common in a demographic group. It does not tell us that this person has a need, that the product is suitable, or that now is the right moment.

**Follow-up**

> What does 12%, 50%, or 63% mean for the client’s decision? If 63% is positive, what does 12% Mortgage mean — negative, neutral, or simply a fact? What decision rule changes ranking or display based on that number?

> A low Mortgage percentage may simply mean that most people are not buying a home. A high Overdraft percentage may mean many people have cash-flow pressure. The same percentage cannot carry the same positive meaning across all products.

---

## 3. Why is the first My Banker message in Investments about Overdraft?

> In the Investments tab, the client is reviewing investment performance. Yet the My Banker card says that 15 of 24 clients like them use Overdraft.
>
> What investment task does this help the client complete? What happened in this client’s investment portfolio that makes Overdraft relevant right now?

**If they say:** “It is cross-sell.”

> Then this is not My Banker guidance inside Investments. It is a credit advertisement injected into an investment journey.

**Follow-up**

> If Cash Loan has the highest peer percentage tomorrow, do we put Cash Loan in the Investments tab instead? If yes, the module has no relationship to the Investments context. It can inject any product anywhere purely because of a percentage.

---

## 4. Are we measuring peer preference, or our own historic sales campaigns?

> Before we treat peer adoption as proof, we need to know what created that adoption.
>
> Perhaps the bank previously ran a strong Cash Loan campaign for this age and income group. Perhaps there were pre-approved offers, RM targeting, favourable pricing, or automatic availability. That activity raises Cash Loan ownership in the group.
>
> Today we use that higher ownership percentage as a reason to recommend Cash Loan again to the same kind of clients.
>
> So how do we separate genuine client need from the effect of our own historic campaigns and sales activity?

**Follow-up**

> Otherwise we are using our own previous sales as evidence that we should create more of the same sales. And once the screen puts Cash Loan first, more exposure creates more ownership, which creates an even higher percentage, which justifies even more exposure.

---

## 5. Does this create incremental sales, or only a new place to claim credit?

> Let us assume the objective is straightforward: we want more credit sales. Then we should test that honestly.
>
> A lead that appears after this screen does not prove that the screen created the sale. The client may already have needed the loan and might have applied through Loans, an RM, a pre-approved offer, or another channel.
>
> We can say the screen was part of the journey. We cannot say it created additional sales unless we compare similar clients who saw the peer experience with similar clients who did not.

**Follow-up**

> If peer analysis does not change the product choice — if Cash Loan is first anyway because it is the most profitable product — then it is only a personalisation layer on top of a normal campaign. What measurable uplift does that layer create compared with exactly the same Cash Loan campaign without peer analysis?

---

## Closing statement

> I am not asking us to sell less. I am asking us to distinguish a genuine client signal from a demographic statistic, a historic campaign effect, and a new attribution field. If peer analysis has a measurable job beyond a normal campaign, let us prove it. If it does not, we should keep the useful investment and product infrastructure and remove the layer that only makes a push look personalised.
